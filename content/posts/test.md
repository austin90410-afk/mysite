# Clinical Case Scenario
A 28-year-old female with a history of mild anxiety presents to the outpatient cardiology clinic for a routine evaluation after being told she had a "heart murmur" during a physical exam for a new job. She is generally asymptomatic but reports occasional episodes of sharp, localized chest pain and palpitations that are unrelated to physical exertion.

Physical Examination:
Vital Signs: BP 118/76 mmHg, HR 72 bpm, RR 14 breaths/min.
Cardiovascular: On auscultation at the apex, there is a mid-systolic click followed by a late systolic murmur.
Dynamic Auscultation: * When the patient stands up from a squatting position, the click occurs earlier in systole and the murmur becomes longer and louder.
When the patient performs a squatting maneuver, the click moves later into systole and the murmur decreases in intensity.
Musculoskeletal: The patient has a slender build with relatively long fingers (arachnodactyly) and mild pectus excavatum.
Laboratory Investigations:
ECG: Normal sinus rhythm with occasional premature ventricular contractions (PVCs).
Transthoracic Echocardiography (TTE):
M-mode/2D: Shows superior displacement of the posterior mitral valve leaflet into the left atrium during systole ($>2$ mm beyond the mitral annular plane).
Morphology: The leaflets appear thickened ($>5$ mm) and redundant, consistent with myxomatous degeneration.
Color Doppler: Trace to mild mitral regurgitation is noted.
What is the most likely diagnosis for this patient?

A) Mitral Stenosis

B) Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM)

C) Mitral Valve Prolapse (MVP)

D) Aortic Stenosis
